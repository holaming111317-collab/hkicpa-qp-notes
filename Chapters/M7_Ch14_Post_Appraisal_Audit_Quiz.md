**Question 1 (MCQ)**

BrightLine Ltd has just completed a capital investment project to automate one of its production lines. The board wants to commission a post-completion audit. Which of the following statements BEST describes the primary purpose of this audit?

A. To finalise and revise the project budget so that the accounting records agree with actual spending
B. To assess whether the project succeeded, met, or fell short of its expectations
C. To evaluate the individual performance of the project manager for reward and promotion purposes
D. To assign responsibility to specific departments for any budget variances that occurred

**Question 2 (MCQ)**

Harbour Manufacturing Ltd is running a two-year capital project divided into six phases. The project sponsor proposes evaluating the project only once, after the full 24 months, arguing that this saves time. Which of the following is the STRONGEST argument, based on post-appraisal audit principles, for instead applying continuous measurement at the end of each phase?

A. Continuous measurement ensures that team leaders can never redefine the project goals
B. Continuous measurement performed at the end of each phase compares progress with budget and timing standards, highlighting emerging problems before they become critical
C. Continuous measurement identifies which team members are idle so that labour can be reallocated
D. Continuous measurement at the end of the project sets new standards that can be applied to future projects

**Question 3 (MCQ)**

Kowloon Foods Ltd invested HK$8 million to launch a new snack brand. Six months after launch, revenues are far below expectations. The marketing director argues the project must continue because "we have already spent HK$8 million and cannot walk away from it." Based on the principles of post-completion audits, which of the following is the CORRECT evaluation of this argument?

A. The argument is valid — abandoning the project would waste the HK$8 million already invested
B. The argument is invalid — capital already invested does not justify continuing a failing project; the decision should be based on whether the future is likely to yield the expected revenues and profits
C. The argument is valid provided the company first conducts a preliminary impact assessment of the launch
D. The argument is invalid — but only because six months is too short a period to judge any capital project
